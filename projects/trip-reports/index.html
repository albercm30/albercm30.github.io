Trip Reports

Wildlife guides spend their evenings writing trip reports by hand. This tool turns a guide's quick notes, voice memos and photos into a polished report with a checked species list, and the guide approves it before anyone sees it.

Problem

Birding and wildlife tour companies send their guests a trip report: a daily narrative, a species list and photos. Guides usually write it by hand after a long day in the field, from memory and a scribbled checklist. It takes an hour that nobody has, species names drift between days, and the list is rarely checked against a standard taxonomy.

How it works

The guide spends under five minutes a day on input: a voice memo, a rough species list and a few photos, sent through a simple web form or, better, a WhatsApp or Telegram bot, since guides are in the field on their phones. The voice memo is transcribed, every species name is matched against a standard taxonomy, and a language model writes each day's narrative in the operator's tone.

The output is a branded web page and PDF with the daily narrative, a species table, a photo gallery and a route map, in English, Spanish or German. Operators can also reuse it for marketing.

Three rules

  • It never adds a species on its own. The narrative only mentions animals the guide actually listed. Anything uncertain (a question mark, "maybe", a name that isn't in the taxonomy) is flagged and waits for the guide.
  • The guide approves everything. Nothing reaches a client until the guide has resolved every flag and pressed approve.
  • The operator owns the data. Each approved report is also structured sighting data (species, count, place, day, observer) that is shared only with the operator's permission.

What the demo does

The demo above is the real workflow in miniature, in English and Spanish. A guide can dictate or type a day the way they would tell a colleague, with no format. The tool picks out the days, dates, times (including “half past six” and “a las siete menos cuarto”), weather, places, species and counts, plus behaviour, highlights and guests. It treats “didn't see it” as a miss rather than a sighting, and flags anything uncertain, heard-only or unknown for the guide to resolve. A live panel shows what it understood while the guide speaks.

It then drafts the narrative in three tones, builds the species table in English or Spanish with scientific names, draws the route, adds the approval step, and exports the sightings as CSV or JSON.

Guide stats

Every approved report can be saved to the guide's logbook. The stats tab turns the logbook into weekly, monthly, yearly and all-time numbers: guiding days, guests, species, individuals, new species, species per day, the best day, how many sightings are backed by a photo, and the share of days on which each species was seen, which is an honest answer to “will we see it?”. A one-page card summarises it for clients and can be printed. The figures are self-reported by the guide, so in the product each one links back to the sightings and photos behind it.

Some parts are simplified so the demo runs for free in the browser: it matches about 390 species instead of the full GBIF taxonomy, understands language with fixed rules instead of a language model, and writes the narrative from templates. Voice dictation uses the browser's own speech recognition where available, instead of Whisper. The demo's sample history is generated and clearly labelled as such.

Business and data

The customer is the operator: a small monthly fee per company, fully remote, with no data licensing problem because the content comes from the operator. The side effect is the interesting part: with permission, every report adds clean, expert-verified sighting records from many countries at once, which is the dataset that trip and chance forecasting is usually missing.

Technical plan

  • Python backend (FastAPI) with Supabase for the database and file storage.
  • Whisper for transcription and GBIF's backbone taxonomy for species names, which has a licence that allows this use.
  • The Claude API for the narrative, with the matched species list as the only source of animals it may mention.
  • WhatsApp or Telegram bot for input, and Stripe for billing.

Honest limits

This is an early-stage project, and the demo is a prototype of the workflow, not the finished product. Its species list is small, its language rules will miss unusual phrasing, the narrative is templated, and the logbook lives only in one browser. Uploaded photos are only shown in the report and are not analysed. Spanish and English are covered; German is planned.
