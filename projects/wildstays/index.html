WildStays

Sleep where the wildlife is. A search engine for lodges, cabins and overnight hides, chosen by the wild animals you can see from them, with an honest chance score for every species and every month.

Problem

Lodges all promise "incredible wildlife", but not which animals, in which month, or how likely you are to actually see them. Choosing where to sleep for a trip built around a species (puffins, wolves, orangutans) means piecing together brochures, forums and trip reports, and none of them tell you how reliable the claim is.

How it works

You search by species, country and month, and stays are ranked by the chance of seeing the animal. Every stay page has a 12-month chance meter per species, an evidence badge, open-data record counts, tips, a map, and a label for where each animal is seen: from your room, on the property, on a guided trip or nearby. The site also has a species finder that understands common names ("grizzly", "big cat") and scientific names, region and month pages, and a six-language interface (English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese).

Species with enough stays get an auto-generated trip planner, printable to PDF and sold for about €2 through a single payment link and webhook. Tours, travel insurance and gear guides are offered as partner links that go through a redirect which counts clicks anonymously.

The chance score

Each species at each stay gets a score per month from three sources: the stay's own documented claim (weight 3), open GBIF records within 5 km in that month (weight 2) and approved guest reports (weight 1 each). Scores map to high, medium or low, and a claim on its own can never reach better than medium. Notes that say an animal is rare lower its score, so a "bonus" species never reads as a likely sighting. After about 20 guest reports, guest data outweighs everything else.

The badge on each animal says where the evidence comes from: publicly documented, host says, records nearby, or guest-confirmed once five guests agree. The model is deliberately plain and explainable, with unit tests, rather than a black box.

Data and quality control

The seed data covers 632 stays in 72 countries and 642 species, compiled from the stays' own websites and travel sources. A monthly GitHub Action refreshes GBIF records and iNaturalist photos (open licences only, credited on every photo). Two checks keep the data honest: an audit that compares every listed animal against GBIF records (within 5 km, only within 25 km, none, or only outside the listed months) and hides unsupported claims, and a link checker that removes the "Visit website" button from broken, parked or spam sites. Both feed an admin quality page.

Ethics policy

Only wild animals. Captive animals, sanctuaries, shows, rides, touching and hand-feeding are excluded, as are stays that attract animals with bait, feeders or feeding stations. Every exclusion and its reason is recorded in a data file, and seeding hides those stays automatically.

Technical decisions

  • Next.js with server actions and Postgres. Next.js 15, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind 4 and Prisma, with Leaflet maps. The same app serves public pages, forms and a password-protected admin area.
  • Built for search. Species, country, region and month pages, a sitemap and schema.org data target queries like "where to stay to see puffins".
  • Privacy and spam by design. Written for a site run from Spain (GDPR and the Spanish e-commerce law): consent checkboxes that aren't pre-ticked, cookieless analytics, a honeypot, a signed time trap and per-visitor rate limiting based on a salted daily hash instead of the IP address.
  • Hosts can claim listings. Seed stays are shown as unclaimed; hosts can claim them or add new places, and an admin approves each claim and sighting report.
  • Automation and tests. CI type-checks, runs the tests, seeds a throwaway database and builds on every push; scheduled jobs handle data sync and privacy clean-up.

Honest limits

It's a pre-launch MVP. The chance scores are estimates from open records, host claims and guest reports, not guarantees, and some map pins are only approximate. No guest statistics are invented: guest scores start at zero and grow only with real reports. Stay descriptions and tips are in English only, and the legal pages are a starting point to be reviewed by a professional before the site earns money.
